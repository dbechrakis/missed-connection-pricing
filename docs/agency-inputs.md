# What only the agency can supply

Public data took this project as far as it can go. The questions that remain are not caveats to be softened; each needs one specific input that an online travel agency has and public data cannot give. This page lists every one, what it enters in the code, how much it matters (measured here), and how to get it. Nothing below is guessed: where this project has no number, the row says so.

| # | Input | Why public data cannot give it | Where it enters | How much it moves the result | How to obtain and check it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Each airline's minimum connection time** by airport and terminal | Published in booking systems (the industry's OAG tables), not in BTS. Public sources agree it varies by airport, airline and terminal, and that some are as short as 25 minutes ([Mile at a Time](https://onemileatatime.com/insights/american-airlines-minimum-connection-time/)); no hub-level figure could be verified | `mcp.v3.model_slack(slack, mct)`: the model is told the buffer above the minimum | Prices set for 45 minutes pay out 0.90× the expected amount at a true 35 and 1.57× at a true 60. Told the buffer, the same frozen model gives 1.11× to 1.13× across 35 to 60 | Pull the table from the booking system. Check by scoring a sample of real connections at their own minimum and comparing realized with expected claims |
| 2 | **What counts as a missed connection for a claim**: a real arrival later than the departure, minus a minimum, or boarding closed | The data has scheduled and actual times, not what a passenger experienced | The label (`missed`) | Moves every probability. The rule used here is arrival plus the minimum later than departure | Compare the rule with rebooking records for the same itineraries |
| 3 | **Attach rate, price response and self-selection** | Nobody has bought this cover | [`mcp.economics`](../src/mcp/economics.py): profit per 100,000 offers shown; the experiment in the [product brief](product-brief.md#7-the-experiment) | Sets whether the cover is worth running at all; see the table in `VALIDATION.md` 5J | The pricing experiment, sized in the brief |
| 4 | **Expense ratio**: commission, payment fees, claims handling | Internal costs; no US regulator publishes a travel-insurance loss ratio ([market evidence](market-evidence.md)) | The target loss ratio (65% here is an assumption) and `mcp.economics` | At the realized loss ratio as sold, each point of expense removes a point of margin | Finance |
| 5 | **The benefit actually paid, and what a disrupted night really costs** | The payout here is the government per-diem rate; real hotel prices on disrupted nights are not public | `mcp.costs`, `claim_cost` | The payout is the product's definition, so the cover's cost per claim is fixed; whether the benefit is enough for travellers is a demand question | Compare the benefit with rooms actually booked for disrupted passengers |
| 6 | **Which trigger to sell**: the missed connection only, or also an onward flight cancelled by weather or air traffic | A product decision | `mcp.outbound`, `mcp.commitments` | Including non-airline onward cancellations adds 20.7% to the events paid (Dallas 32%, Chicago 21%, Charlotte 25%, Atlanta 16%, Denver 9%). Frontier, the one named airline that does not commit to hotels, would be paid on any miss | Decide, then price the added events with the same machinery |
| 7 | **Exposure limits**: a cap per hub-week or a reinsurance quote | Needs a counterparty | Stop-loss cost in `VALIDATION.md` 5I | A weekly stop-loss at 100% would have cost at least 8.6% of premium as sold, 11.5% at 90%, over 114 weeks, before loading | Ask a reinsurer with the weekly series in `results/v3_rolling_weeks.csv` |
| 8 | **Legal form**: insurance or a benefit | Jurisdiction-specific | Not in the code | Can end the project | Legal |

**Order of work for an agency.** Rows 1 and 2 first: they change prices and need no customers. Row 6 next, because it changes what is being sold. Row 3 last and only once rows 1 to 6 give a price worth testing.
