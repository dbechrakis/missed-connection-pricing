The gap cover: the hotel night the airline won't pay for

US airlines already rebook a missed connection for free, and pay the hotel when an overnight disruption is their fault. The night after a weather or air-traffic disruption is the gap nobody covers. This cover pays the official government hotel-and-meals rate for the hub and month, only when the connection is missed, the next flight is the next day, and the delay is not coded to the airline. (Model v3, connections paired the way tickets are sold. Pre-registered for September to December 2026, results not in yet.)

What this does not tell you

  • Connections are synthetic. They are every same-airline pairing of an arriving and a departing flight at five US hubs; the data does not say which passengers actually connected.
  • In the v1 calculator, the costs, the 65% target and the 10% cap are assumptions ($ to rebook, $ more if the next flight is the next day). The results barely depend on the dollar amounts, but the prices do.
  • Demand is untested. On one ticket an airline usually rebooks a missed connection for free, so the offer has to cover something extra. How many people would buy, and at what price, needs an experiment (see the product brief).
  • No seasonal price. One year of history could not separate seasons from one-off events, so the model has no month term and these prices are the same all year.
  • Five airports, same-airline connections, US domestic, data to August 2026.
  • The calculator at the top shows v1 prices. A later audit found airlines already pay for much of what v1 covers, which led to the gap cover with official hotel rates and a sourced 5% cap. Its probabilities have no month term, so only the payout moves with the month; whole months have run 20 to 40% above or below the price.
